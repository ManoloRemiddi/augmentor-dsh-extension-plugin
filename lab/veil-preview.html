The quiet science of weather

A field guide to clouds, fronts, and the strange mathematics of storms.

Long before instruments, people read the sky the way sailors still read the sea: as a surface with depth. The low scud streaming across a winter evening is not merely information about tomorrow — it is the visible edge of a three-dimensional machine of heat and vapor, turning above the ground.

Why frost looks alive

Frost forms in fractals because water, freezing, has opinions. Each crystal branches in six because the ice lattice is hexagonal, and the branches branch again because the tips of the crystals grow faster than their flanks. The result is a surface that records its entire weather history in a single flake — a document written in the language of diffusion and diffusion-limited growth.
